# Year in Review 2026 — Open Questions on National Impact
### Response to the Briefing Document (CD_NR, 14 May 2026) + asks of Editorial
*Design unit working paper, June 2026. Companion to `05_national_impact_mapping.md`.
Purpose: surface the questions that must be answered during the June "content
structure and digital concept" phase so that national-impact content is
generated — not retrofitted — and so the spatial channel can run on the same
content system as the publication.*

---

## A. Our position on Recommendation 1 (Option 1)

**We support Option 1.** A strategy-led thematic narrative emphasizing
interconnection and national contribution is exactly the structure our
national-impact mapping calls for. The hero-story requirement matches our
golden-threads approach. We should say this clearly before raising anything.

We then raise five questions on it:

**A1. "National priorities" — against which framework, explicitly?**
The briefing uses "national priorities" and "national development" throughout
but never names the spine. QNV 2030 (four pillars)? NDS3 2024–2030 (seven
strategic national outcomes)? Sector strategies (National Cancer Strategy,
Food Security Strategy 2030)? These are different vocabularies with different
owners. **Our recommendation: NDS3's seven national outcomes as the canonical
alignment layer** — it is the framework ministers are personally accountable
to, it has published targets, and it is current (2024–2030). QF's themes
remain the narrative structure; NDS3 outcomes become the explicit tagging and
claims layer underneath. Without this decision, "alignment with national
priorities" stays a sentiment, not a structure — which is precisely what
went wrong last year.

**A2. "Proof points where available" — we ask to flip the burden.**
As drafted, this phrase makes evidence optional. For the primary audience it
cannot be: a national-impact claim without a proof point is a liability in a
room of officials who know their own numbers. Proposed editorial rule: **every
claim of contribution to a national outcome carries a proof point; claims that
can't be evidenced are reframed as activity, not impact.** This single rule is
the difference between a reputational tool and a reputational risk.

**A3. Where does Cohesive Society live if Social Progress is dissolved?**
Option 1 names four priority areas (Progressive Education, AI, Precision
Health, Sustainability) and interweaves Social Progress. But in our mapping,
Social Progress is the theme that maps *most directly* onto a full NDS3
outcome — Cohesive Society — and carries the strongest sovereign-identity
material (Arabic, Fanar, family policy, Al-Mujadilah). If the NDS3 spine is
adopted (A1), this resolves itself: the evidence has a home under the Cohesive
Society outcome regardless of QF's thematic structure. If A1 is not adopted,
we need an explicit answer for where this evidence aggregates — otherwise the
interweaving disperses it.

**A4. Hero stories — selected by what criterion?**
"Linked to major achievements or milestones" selects for press value. We
propose selecting (or at least weighting) by **cross-outcome density**: the
stories that light up three or more national outcomes at once (the newborn
screened by QScan; Fanar; the PGx heart patient; the Awsaj Prime graduate).
These do the connect-the-dots work by themselves and serve both the
publication and the spatial layer.

**A5. Success measures — how is primary-audience engagement actually
measured?** The scorecard lists "reach and quality of engagement" and
"leadership and reputational value." For tertiary audiences these are web
analytics. For the *primary* audience — government and policy stakeholders —
what is the instrument? Page views cannot tell us whether a minister engaged.
(This question opens the door to the spatial channel's Audience Intelligence:
dwell, attention, and delegation-level engagement at trade fairs and the
visitor center are measurable in a way a website visit by this audience never
will be.)

## B. The unscoped spatial component — how we position it

Stated plainly, the briefing contains a structural tension: it commits to a
**digital-only publication** while naming **government and policy
stakeholders as the primary audience**. We do not believe this audience will
have significant engagement with a web experience, however good. Their
engagement happens in rooms: at trade fairs (where ministers are mandated to
visit national booths) and at QF HQ (where the visitor center receives
official delegations).

**We are not asking to reopen the publication decision.** The February
digital publication remains the canonical product. We are proposing that the
same content system renders to a second surface: a spatial experience —
trade-fair deployment and a permanent HQ visitor-center installation, same
system, different topology. One ontology, two renderers.

The ask attached to this is small but time-critical (see C9): content must be
authored as **structured, tagged story data**, not flat prose. If June's
"content structure" phase defines prose templates only, the spatial channel
becomes an expensive retrofit. If it defines a story schema, the spatial
channel is nearly free at the content level — and so is every future surface.

## C. The master list: open questions for showing impact at the national level

Grouped, each with the ask of Editorial. C1–C5 are the load-bearing ones.

**C1. NDS3 targets — access, clearance, and governance.**
Can we obtain and quote the official NDS3 targets and KPIs alongside QF
figures ("NDS3 targets X by 2030; QF contributed Y in 2026")? Who validates
the juxtaposition — and does any side-by-side with official national targets
require National Planning Council (or equivalent) sign-off? *Overclaiming
against national targets is the single fatal error in this room.*
**Ask:** Editorial to source the official NDS3 target set (npc.qa), confirm
clearance rules, and own a validated claims register.

**C2. Attribution standard — "contributed to" vs. "delivered."**
What is the house rule for causal language? The 2025 DIFI material models it
well ("five policy recommendations had evidence of implementation") — careful,
defensible. We need that standard codified: when may we say QF *delivered* a
national result, when *contributed*, when *supported*?
**Ask:** a one-page attribution style rule in the delivery framework's
editorial principles.

**C3. Time series — the compounding story needs history.**
The 30-year compounding narrative (Beat 2 of the emotional architecture)
requires per-year data: graduates per year since 1996, QF's share of national
research output over time, cumulative genome records, cumulative patents.
2025 templates give snapshots only.
**Ask:** Editorial to source historic series from Strategy / the Alumni
Impact Study / entity records — even 5–6 anchor years per metric is enough to
draw the curve.

**C4. The ministry dimension — taggable, and permissible?**
Ministers think in mandates. Can content be tagged by NDS3 outcome *and* by
partner ministry (MoPH, MoEHE, MCIT, Defence, Sports & Youth, Foreign
Affairs — all already present in the 2025 material)? And is naming ministries
in a recognition layer protocol-acceptable, or do we design the fallback
(sector-level recognition)?
**Ask:** Editorial/Comms to confirm the protocol position early; we design to
whichever answer comes back.

**C5. Fiscal Sustainability — who owns the prevention-economics narrative?**
NDS3 outcome 2 is QF's quietest but most ministerial story: cost avoided is
revenue protected (90% prediabetes prevention, PGx-reduced hospitalizations,
newborn screening). It needs health-economics framing — even conservative,
sourced cost-avoidance figures.
**Ask:** Editorial to commission/obtain prevention-economics proof points
from QPHI / Sidra / WISH, or we consciously drop outcome 2 from the claims
layer.

**C6. Government Excellence — the policy ledger.**
The evidence→policy pipeline (DIFI policies implemented, genomics in the
national curriculum, the digital-addiction task force, Fanar serving
agencies) is scattered across entity reports and is the most under-told
national story.
**Ask:** Editorial to maintain a running 2026 ledger: which QF evidence
became which national policy, regulation, or curriculum, with dates.

**C7. Partnerships — what is the proof unit?**
The briefing emphasizes partnerships at national/regional/global level. As
impact, what do we count — number, depth, co-delivery with ministries,
external capital attracted (Arbor, Gates, Mastercard)?
**Ask:** a defined partnership-impact metric, or partnerships stay
descriptive.

**C8. Baselines for "firsts" and superlatives.**
"Qatar's first pediatric HSCT," "first quantum lab," "first BSAI" — firsts
are strong ministerial currency but must be verifiable.
**Ask:** Editorial to verify and source every "first/largest/only" claim in
a claims register (shared with C1).

**C9. Structured content — author into the schema, not into prose. [TIME-CRITICAL]**
For the Living Archive, the publication, *and* the spatial channel to run
from one source of truth, stories must be authored as tagged nodes:
`{qf_theme, nds3_outcome(s), qnv_pillar, entity, ministry_links,
human_thread, metric + so-that chain, era}`. The 108-story 2025 archive needs
only two added tag dimensions; 2026 content should be born this way.
**Ask:** the June content-structure definition adopts this schema (we
provide it — section 6 of the mapping doc). This is the one ask that cannot
slip past June.

**C10. Arabic-first, with official terminology.**
NDS3 outcomes have official government Arabic phrasing. Alignment claims must
use the state's own terms, not retranslations — and the briefing's
January-Arabization step implies translation-last, which contradicts both
the Social Progress message and the audience.
**Ask:** Arabic terminology locked from the official NDS3 Arabic text at
schema stage, not in January.

**C11. Data timeline vs. the spatial calendar.**
The briefing's flow (final data in January, publication in February) suits
the web product. The trade-fair calendar may not wait: which fairs is QF
committed to in late 2026/early 2027, and do any precede February?
**Ask:** Events/Comms to share the fair calendar; if a deployment precedes
publication, Editorial defines a staged data-drop (provisional 2026 figures,
clearly versioned) — the OS is designed to ingest updates, so this is a
workflow question, not a build question.

**C12. The measurement loop back to Editorial.**
The spatial layer's analytics (which stories hold ministerial attention,
where delegations dwell) are next year's editorial intelligence — the
"Audience Intelligence" loop, on-device and identity-blind.
**Ask:** agree now that spatial engagement data feeds the YiR 2027 content
prioritization, closing the scorecard gap in A5.

## D. The one-paragraph summary for the room

We endorse the recommended strategy-led approach. To make "alignment with
national priorities" real rather than rhetorical, we ask for five decisions in
June: (1) NDS3's seven outcomes as the explicit alignment spine; (2) every
national claim carries a proof point — evidence is mandatory, not "where
available"; (3) content authored as structured, tagged story data so one
content system serves the publication, the Living Archive, and the spatial
channel we will propose for the primary audience at trade fairs and the HQ
visitor center; (4) Editorial to lead sourcing of the national-impact
evidence pack — official NDS3 targets and clearance rules, historic time
series, prevention economics, the policy ledger, verified firsts; (5) Arabic
NDS3 terminology locked at the start, not translated at the end. The briefing
names government and policy stakeholders as the primary audience; these five
decisions are what make the content capable of reaching them.
